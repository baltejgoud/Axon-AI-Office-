Build the hiring loop for one role so every candidate is judged on the same evidence, against what the job actually needs. Work through the steps in order; each one feeds the next.

## 1. Outcomes before requirements

Ask the hiring manager (or the user) what this person must **achieve in their first 6–12 months**: three to five outcomes, each observable ("cut onboarding time for new customers from 3 weeks to 1"), not activities ("manage onboarding"). If the user can't answer, use `ask_colleague` to get the view of the specialist closest to the work.

## 2. Scorecard

Turn the outcomes into four to six **competencies** that predict them. For each give:
- what "strong" looks like, in behaviour you could observe;
- what "weak" looks like;
- how it will be assessed (which interview, exercise or reference check).

Separate **must-haves** from **nice-to-haves**. Challenge any requirement that isn't tied to an outcome — degree, years of experience, a specific tool — because it narrows the pool without predicting success.

## 3. Job ad

Lead with the outcomes and why the work matters, then the must-haves, then practicalities (location, pay range if it can be shared, process and timeline). Use plain, inclusive language: no "rockstar" or "ninja", no gendered wording, and no list of twenty nice-to-haves that discourages qualified people from applying.

## 4. Structured interviews

Assign each competency to exactly one stage so no interviewer duplicates another. For each stage write:
- two or three **questions** — behavioural ("Tell me about a time you…") or situational ("What would you do if…");
- **follow-ups** that probe for specifics: what *they* did, the result, what they'd change;
- a **1–4 rating guide** with an example answer at each level.

Every candidate for the role gets the same questions in the same order.

## 5. Debrief

Interviewers score **independently, before** discussing, and write the evidence behind each score. In the debrief, go competency by competency: evidence first, then scores. Decide against the scorecard, not on overall impressions or "culture fit". Record the decision and the reason.

## Pitfalls

- Deciding in the first five minutes and then looking for confirmation.
- Letting one strong opinion set the room — hence scoring before discussion.
- Unstructured "chat" interviews: pleasant, but a weak predictor.
- Questions about age, family plans, health, religion or other protected characteristics: never ask them, and check local employment law before finalising the process.
